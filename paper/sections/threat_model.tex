\section{Motivation and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}
The trusted boundary contains an organization's operational data sources and
the transformation layer that can consult field annotations. The external
LLM or coding/RCA agent receives a rendered troubleshooting question and a
transformed \texttt{ModelView}, not the complete incident object. We initially
model the provider as honest-but-curious: it performs the requested analysis
but should not receive avoidable identity or structural information. We do
not claim protection against a compromised provider or an adversarial
operator with control of the trusted host.

The assets in scope include direct identity, operational and linkable
identifiers, original format, quasi-identifying timestamps, and the
relationships among these fields. A classified \texttt{secret} or
\texttt{never\_release} literal is categorically non-releasable: the offline
analysis fails closed if it occurs verbatim anywhere in the released view.
No current development field carries that annotation, so this is a policy
and synthetic-test property, not a measured zero secret-exposure rate.
Metadata such as record counts and unannotated timestamps can remain visible
even when annotated identities are changed.

This first-paper threat model does not replace access control or address
prompt injection, malicious autonomous agents, poisoning, malicious
insiders, cryptographic provider-side guarantees, or a complete legal or
regulatory analysis. A mistaken sensitivity annotation is itself a residual
exposure route. The exposure measurements in Section~\ref{sec:privacy} concern
what appears in the model-visible representation. They do not estimate an
adversary's probability of re-identifying a person from auxiliary data.
