\section{Incident-Family Design}
\label{sec:families}
\paragraph{Incident 001: repeated payment effect.}
A deadline or timeout precedes re-arrival of the same logical request.
The operation executor lacks an effective idempotency control and records
two successful effects. The frozen tuple is
\texttt{duplicate\_effect},
\texttt{timeout\_or\_deadline},
\texttt{retry\_or\_rearrival},
\texttt{missing\_or\_ineffective\_idempotency},
\texttt{operation\_executor}.
The ingress component observes arrivals but does not own the missing
suppression control; component ownership is a distinct and potentially
model-sensitive judgement. The minimum diagnostic structure includes
same-request equality across sources, two distinct effects, successful
states, and event order. The customer's literal identity is not needed.
This family tests whether relationship-preserving pseudonyms can retain
RCA utility after direct identity is changed.

\paragraph{Incident 002: shape-sensitive malformed external reference.}
A provisioning operation with an unexpected external-reference shape
reaches an exact lookup, misses, and yields no effect because domain-level
validation or normalization is inadequate. The oracle tuple is:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
missing\_effect; malformed\_or\_unexpected\_input;\\
no\_follow\_on\_action;\\
validation\_or\_input\_handling\_failure; operation\_executor.
\end{quote}
Controls and benign lookup misses make a simple miss or latency clue
insufficient; the target's structural deviation from a healthy template is
the intended differentiator. Six generator-side defect classes are represented,
including separator, width, case-class, and truncation changes. A hidden
\texttt{leading\_zero\_loss} construction is described to the evaluator only
as an observable leading-position numeric-run deletion: visible structure
cannot reveal the literal value of the lost digit. Format-preserving
pseudonymization retains the structural signature that HMAC and ordinary
tokens do not. Retention is not sufficient by itself to ensure a correct
model answer.

\paragraph{Incident 003 v3: temporal causal isolation.}
The target execution is still active when compensation begins after a
deadline. Its later successful original effect coexists with the compensated
workflow state; correct fencing or serialization would prevent this
\texttt{incorrect\_concurrency\_control} mechanism. The temporal twin has
the same non-temporal terminal, source, event-kind, linkage, and message
signatures, but its successful effect is committed before the deadline and
compensation follows in serialized order. Its mechanism is
\texttt{incorrect\_state\_transition}: the lifecycle edge is invalid even
without concurrent actors. A third operation is a safe control. The
target/twin questions name different operation IDs but present otherwise
identical evidence. Offline audits show matching non-temporal projections,
structural isomorphism of normalized ablated views, and conflicting
mechanism oracles across 200 development constructions. This is stronger
isolation than contrasting two unrelated transformed incidents; it is
still a targeted construction audit rather than proof against every
learned cue.

Raw and relative-time representations retain the order and overlap that
distinguish the queried mechanisms. Temporal ablation replaces timestamps
with non-parseable markers and removes residual chronological log ordering.
Both roles still have the same non-temporal evidence; under ablation the
temporal proof is unavailable rather than merely harder to read.
