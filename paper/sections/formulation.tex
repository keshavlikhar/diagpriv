\section{Problem Formulation and Diagnostic Invariants}
\label{sec:formulation}
Let $D$ denote raw operational evidence, $Q$ the troubleshooting question,
and $A$ a trusted field-annotation policy. A transformation condition $T_c$
produces $D'_c=T_c(D;A)$ for release. A future task-aware selector could choose
$c$ from $(D,Q,A)$, but no such selector is evaluated here. The frozen prompt
renders only $(Q,D'_c)$ for the external model. The model returns a
five-dimensional RCA tuple
\[
R=(o,t,a,m,k),
\]
where $o$ is observed outcome, $t$ the precipitating trigger, $a$ the
subsequent action, $m$ the failure mechanism, and $k$ the component owning
that mechanism. Exact RCA requires every field to equal the frozen oracle.
Evidence support is a separate judgement about cited visible records.

For task $Q$, let $I_Q=\{i_1,\ldots,i_n\}$ be declared diagnostic relations
over the evidence. Examples are \texttt{request\_id} equality across a
structured row and a log, distinctness of two payment effects, the
fixed-width class of an external reference, and strict
\texttt{completion < compensation} ordering. An offline audit asks whether
each relation is genuinely preserved in $D'_c$, broken, preserved only through
degenerate collapse, or intentionally discarded because literal identity
is nonessential. These statuses are not a proof that a model will use the
relation correctly. A shape-preserving condition, for example, can preserve
every declared Incident 002 structural invariant while the model still
misclassifies a development case.

The conceptual objective is to retain the relations needed for the
diagnosis while reducing unnecessary release:
\[
\text{seek }T_c\text{ such that }i_j(D,Q)\text{ remains assessable in }D'_c
\quad\text{for essential }i_j,
\]
subject to explicit non-release rules. This is a research objective rather
than an optimized scalar function. Utility is reported as the vector of exact
RCA, individual dimension accuracy, paired mechanism discrimination, and
evidence support. Privacy is reported as a separate exposure vector.
Neither equality of model answers nor an invariant audit implies a formal
sufficient statistic, exact reasoning invariance, or a privacy guarantee.

Incident-specific classifications distinguish \emph{essential} relations
needed to identify the cause, \emph{supportive} relations that strengthen a
case without defining it, and \emph{discardable} facts such as a fictional
customer's literal name. For Incident 001 the literal customer identity can
be discarded while same-request equality remains essential. Incident 002
requires reference structure but not the reference's literal characters.
Incident 003 requires the relative order and overlap of execution,
deadline, and compensation, not the original calendar date.
