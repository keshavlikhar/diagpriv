Investigate the supplied operational evidence and identify the most likely causal explanation for the reported incident. Base your diagnosis only on the supplied evidence.

Choose exactly one canonical value for each categorical field. Apply these dimension rules independently:

- observed_outcome: the externally meaningful failure or contradictory state that occurred.
- trigger: the event or condition that directly initiated the causal sequence. A later reaction belongs under subsequent_action.
- subsequent_action: the first post-trigger action necessary to propagate the failure. Use no_follow_on_action when no distinct later action is necessary.
- failure_mechanism: the most proximal defective or missing system control that allowed the sequence to produce the outcome.
- affected_component: the architectural role that owns that control, not a normal participant or symptom reporter.

Allowed observed_outcome values and definitions:

- degraded_performance: The service completes work but with materially reduced performance.
- delayed_effect: The intended effect occurs later than its required time.
- duplicate_effect: One logical operation produces more than one externally meaningful effect.
- incorrect_effect: An effect occurs, but its content, target, or value is wrong.
- inconsistent_state: Recorded states or externally visible effects contradict one another.
- missing_effect: A required externally meaningful effect does not occur.
- partial_effect: Only part of a required multi-part effect occurs.
- service_unavailable: The service cannot provide its intended operation.
- unauthorized_effect: An effect occurs without the required authorization or policy permission.
- unknown_outcome: The supplied evidence does not establish a more specific outcome.

Allowed trigger values and definitions:

- concurrent_activity: Overlapping activity directly initiates the causal sequence.
- dependency_error: An upstream or downstream dependency error initiates the causal sequence.
- deployment_or_configuration_change: A deployment or configuration change initiates the causal sequence.
- malformed_or_unexpected_input: Input with an unexpected value or structure initiates the causal sequence.
- operator_action: A human operational action initiates the causal sequence.
- process_restart_or_failover: A restart, crash recovery, or failover initiates the causal sequence.
- resource_exhaustion: Exhaustion of a bounded resource initiates the causal sequence.
- scheduled_event: A time-scheduled job or event initiates the causal sequence.
- timeout_or_deadline: A timeout or deadline crossing initiates the causal sequence. A later retry, replay, or compensation belongs under subsequent_action.
- traffic_or_load_change: A material change in traffic or load initiates the causal sequence.
- unknown_trigger: The supplied evidence does not establish a more specific trigger.

Allowed subsequent_action values and definitions:

- compensation_or_rollback: A compensation or rollback action follows the trigger and propagates the failure.
- concurrent_duplicate_submission: A separate overlapping submission of the same intended work follows or accompanies the trigger.
- failover_or_reroute: A failover or reroute follows the trigger and propagates the failure.
- manual_reprocessing: Human-initiated reprocessing follows the trigger.
- message_redelivery_or_replay: Automated delivery infrastructure redelivers or replays a message after the trigger.
- no_follow_on_action: No distinct post-trigger action is necessary to produce the outcome.
- retry_or_rearrival: A retry or repeated request arrival follows the trigger.
- scheduled_reprocessing: Scheduled reprocessing follows the trigger.
- unknown_follow_on_action: The supplied evidence does not establish a more specific subsequent action.

Allowed failure_mechanism values and definitions:

- authorization_or_policy_failure: Authorization or policy enforcement is absent, incorrect, or bypassed.
- configuration_or_routing_error: Incorrect configuration or routing directs the system into the failure.
- data_integrity_failure: Stored or transmitted data violates a required integrity constraint.
- dependency_contract_failure: A component relies on dependency behavior that violates or is absent from the dependency contract.
- incorrect_concurrency_control: Overlapping actors can both proceed or commit because coordination is absent or ineffective. This includes missing or ineffective fencing, locking, serialization, cancellation enforcement, compare-and-set, or version checking.
- incorrect_retry_policy: The decision to retry, its timing, or its retry bounds are themselves defective. Use this only when the retry decision remains defective assuming effective idempotency.
- incorrect_state_transition: A lifecycle edge is allowed from an invalid source state or without a required state precondition, and the edge remains invalid under serialized execution.
- missing_or_ineffective_idempotency: Repeated execution of one logical identity is not suppressed or safely replayed, and correct deduplication or safe replay alone would prevent the duplicate outcome.
- non_atomic_commit_or_rollback: A logically indivisible multi-part commit or rollback can partially apply during serialized execution; it does not require overlapping actors.
- resource_management_failure: Resource allocation, release, capacity, or lifetime management is defective.
- stale_or_inconsistent_data: A decision uses stale, mutually inconsistent, or insufficiently synchronized data.
- validation_or_input_handling_failure: Input validation, normalization, parsing, or lookup handling is missing or incorrect.
- unknown_failure_mechanism: The supplied evidence does not establish a more specific mechanism.

Failure-mechanism decision guidance:

- When repeated executions share one logical identity and effective deduplication or safe replay alone would prevent a duplicate outcome, choose missing_or_ineffective_idempotency over generic retry or concurrency framing.
- Choose incorrect_concurrency_control when the failure requires overlapping actors and correct fencing, locking, serialization, cancellation enforcement, compare-and-set, or version checking would prevent it.
- Choose incorrect_state_transition when an invalid lifecycle edge remains invalid under serialized execution.
- When a state inconsistency exists only because concurrent work was not controlled, choose incorrect_concurrency_control over incorrect_state_transition.
- Choose non_atomic_commit_or_rollback when a serialized, logically indivisible multi-part operation can partially apply without concurrent actors.
- Choose incorrect_retry_policy only when the retry decision remains defective assuming effective idempotency.

Allowed affected_component values and definitions:

- client_or_caller: The calling client owns the defective or missing control.
- control_plane: A control-plane component owns the defective or missing control.
- external_dependency: An external dependency owns the defective or missing control.
- identity_or_policy_service: An identity, authorization, or policy service owns the defective or missing control.
- message_delivery_layer: The queue, broker, or delivery layer owns the defective or missing control.
- multiple_components: The mechanism is jointly owned by multiple components and no single owner is defensible.
- observability_pipeline: Telemetry collection, processing, or presentation owns the defective or missing control.
- operation_executor: The component applying the domain operation or effect owns the defective or missing control.
- request_ingress: The ingress, gateway, or request-admission layer owns the defective or missing control.
- scheduler: The scheduler or timed-job system owns the defective or missing control.
- state_store: The persistent state store or its consistency control owns the defect.
- workflow_orchestrator: The workflow coordinator owns the defective transition, timing, compensation, or coordination control.
- unknown_component: The supplied evidence does not establish a more specific owning component.
